Over the last fifteen years, I have seen dozens of note-taking apps and publishing platforms rise and fall. Each one promised a revolutionary way to organize your brain. They had proprietary databases, real-time syncing APIs, and slick marketing pages.

And eventually, they all either got acquired, changed their pricing model, or shut down entirely.

## The humble text file

Contrast that with a `.txt` or `.md` file created thirty years ago. 

You can double-click it on any computer on earth today—Windows, Mac, Linux, or a command-line terminal on a Raspberry Pi—and read every single word without converting a database or paying a subscription.

```text
2026-10-04: Sitting here at the desk, thinking about what lasts.
```

Plain text is indestructible.

## Keeping it close to the chest

When you write in an app that syncs to someone else's server, you are a guest in their house. When you write into files on your local drive and push them with Git, you own the building.

That is why this entire blog is constructed from plain text files:

- No dynamic SQL database to corrupt.
- No backend servers to keep patched.
- Just static HTML generated from honest words.

If the world loses power tomorrow, my thoughts are still safely recorded on my local drive.
